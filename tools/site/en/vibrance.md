the classic editor's **Vibrance**, matched by black-box measurement rather than
approximated. The same calibration chart was pushed through the classic editor 26.10 and through this
operator; the pixel-level comparison is in the acceptance section.

- GEGL id: `lb:vibrance`
- Menu: `Colors → SuperMrWang → Color → Vibrance`
- Type: `GeglOperationPointFilter` (per-pixel, no neighbourhood)
- Parameters: `vibrance` (−100…100), `protect_reds`, `compensate_lightness`

## Vibrance only — temperature and tint are elsewhere

the classic editor 2026 (27.0) renamed the Vibrance layer to "Color and vibrance" and added
**temperature / tint** sliders, which action-wise are **one white-balance layer
followed by the old Vibrance**.

This project does not fuse the two into one operator: `lb:color-temperature-tint` owns
temperature/tint and `lb:vibrance` does Vibrance alone. To reproduce "Color and
vibrance", stack two layers in order:

```
layer / filter stack:  Color Temperature & Tint (lb:color-temperature-tint)  →  Vibrance (lb:vibrance)
```

For 2025's plain Vibrance, delete the lower layer. Why split it:

- each operator has one slider's worth of responsibility, so there is never a question
  of "does temperature act on the whole layer or only this segment";
- the order is expressed by the layer stack (the classic editor's order is white balance first —
  measured median difference 1.4 code values; reversed it is 36), and changing it means
  reordering layers instead of unticking a box;
- the white-balance result is **bit-identical** to running
  `lb:color-temperature-tint` alone, so there are not two implementations drifting
  apart.

## Algorithm

Convert to HSV on **sRGB encoded values** (`R~G~B~A float`, the perceptual domain, not
linear light) and derive an increment from the source saturation S:

```
ΔS = A(v) · S^p(v) · (1−S)^q(v) · r(H,S,v) · c(L)
S' = min(1, S + ΔS)
```

| Term | Meaning | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| `A(v)`, `p(v)`, `q(v)` | amplitude and the curvature at both ends, per step of v | 16-bit calibration in steps of 5, 20 steps |
| `r(H,S,v)` | extra suppression in the red band (within 40° of red), fading linearly out from 26° to 40° | ratio of H0 to H120 at the same v |
| `c(L)` | lightness correction: give less to light colours (0.72× at L=80, 1.39× at L=20) | the L ladder of segment E |

RGB reconstruction: keep max fixed and scale the other channels by `S'/S` —
`out = mx − (mx − c)·S'/S`. Since `mx − mn = S·mx`, whenever `S' ≤ 1` the minimum
channel lands exactly in `[0, mx]`, so **the maths cannot leave the gamut** and no
clipping or gamut mapping is needed.

The negative direction (v<0) is **a different mechanism**, not a mirror of the
positive one: measurements show S drops *and* V drops with it, and there is no hue
term, so it uses two tables (`S'` and `V'/V`), also bilinearly interpolated over
(v, S).

The tables live in `vibrance-tables.h`, generated by `tools/gen_vibrance_tables.py`
from `vibrance-cal/vibrance_model.json` — change the JSON and regenerate, never
hand-edit.

## Performance

`v` is constant over the image, so every curve that varies with v is folded into one
row (11 samples) in `prepare()`, leaving `process()` with a 1D lookup and two `powf`.
The 1080×880 calibration chart renders in about 0.2 s through the gegl CLI including
PNG encode/decode.

## Acceptance

`python tools/check_vibrance_ps.py` renders the chart through the gegl CLI and compares
it with the classic editor's real export, pixel by pixel (0–255 code values; quantisation alone
is ±0.5):

| v | Median | 95% | Max | Mean | Exactly equal to the classic editor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.00 | 100% |
| +60 | 0 | 4 | 7 | 0.83 | 63.5% |
| +100 | 0 | 8 | 15 | 1.58 | 68.1% |
| −60 | 0 | 5 | 17 | 0.82 | 65.1% |
| −100 | 0 | 7 | 30 | 1.10 | 61.9% |

`v=0` is bit-identical, so the colour pipeline (linear↔encoded round trip) does not
secretly touch pixels. The C implementation matches the Python reference in
`vibrance-cal/vibrance_ref.py` pixel for pixel (≤1 code, pure rounding).

The two-layer "Color and vibrance" acceptance against the classic editor 2026, and the accuracy of the
temperature/tint part, are in `ops/color-temperature-tint/README.md` — median/p90/p99/max
over seven v/t/n combinations, plus the known deviation on the diagonal where
temperature ≤ −50 and tint ≥ +38 and the classic editor clips hard.

## Known deviations

The fit residual only holds at the sampled points (segment A's ΔS residual is 0.41 pp
at v=+100); on a real image the bulk of the error sits in two places **the calibration
grid did not cover**:

1. **Orange and skin tones near H≈17° inside the red band (segment B, L62)**: `r` was
   fitted on H0 only, and the classic editor suppresses H17 harder. At v=+60 that segment is off by a
   median of 2.9 code values, 8.4 at v=+100 (max 14.8).
2. **The negative direction's lightness change on light colours (segment E)**: the
   `V'/V` table was fitted at L=50 only, and at L=80 the classic editor presses much less than the
   table does (up to 29 code values there — a visible darkening in sRGB).

Neither is an implementation bug: the model has too few dimensions (six hue samples,
five lightness steps). The fix would be a probe v2 — an L×S grid plus a 5° hue sweep —
and a refit of `r(H,S,v)` and of the negative `V'(S,L,v)`.

## Relation to `lb:vibrance-hyper`

`vibrance-hyper` is an **appearance model** in Lab (hyperbolic chroma mask, skin window,
gamut mapping): pleasant to use with explainable parameters, but not guaranteed to
match the classic editor numerically. `vibrance` is the **numerical reproduction**: it matches
the classic editor, but has no artistic parameters. Both exist; pick by need.
